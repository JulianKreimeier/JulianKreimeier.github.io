Multimodal Augmented Reality Guidance for Object Localization and Wayfinding in Visually Challenging Environments
R. Damouni, M. H. Ali, H. Schieber, J.Kreimeier, D.Roth, I. Shimshoni and S.F.A. Szpiro
International Symposium on Mixed and Augmented Reality (ISMAR), 2026

Abstract IEEE

Object localization is difficult in cluttered environments, even more for people with visual impairments like tunnel vision. Augmented reality and spatial audio offer promising avenues for assistive cueing, yet little is known about how auditory, visual, and combined cues compare in guiding object search in real-life conditions. Using a video see through head-mounted display and camera-based object localization, we compared these conditions in a user study. Six participants searched objects in an everyday live environment. Our system provides distance-adaptive, spatially localized guidance toward the objects. We simulated tunnel vision and revealed the potential of dual-modality spatialized cues in supporting daily tasks.


Multi-camera AR Guidance System for Surgical Instrument Handling and Assembly: Investigating Workload and Efficiency
S. Li, J.Kreimeier, H. Schieber, D. Mueller, B. Kainz, R. von Eisenhart-Rothe and D. Roth
29th International Conference on Medical Image Computing and Computer Assisted Intervention (MICCAI), 2026

Abstract Springer

The handling and assembly of instruments during surgery impose high cognitive demands on scrub nurses, particularly when instruments are unfamiliar. We present a supporting guidance system for surgical instrumentation combining multi-camera marker-free 6D pose estimation with augmented reality (AR) in-situ visualization on a head-mounted display. Pose estimation and consecutive camera calibration are achieved through known objects. Our 6D pose estimation is purely trained on synthetic data, aiming for better generalizability and real-world applicability. The AR guidance displays tooltip localization cues and step-wise assembly animations. Via gaze-based selection and a foot pedal, users can switch assembly steps in intraoperative use. In a technical evaluation, we outperform state-of-the-art 6D pose estimation. A user study with 29 scrub nurses conducted in a surgical simulation of knee arthroplasty compares our system against a paper manual. Our AR system significantly reduced the perceived workload. Objectively, AR guidance reduced task completion time by 21.3% (4.76 minutes). Specifically, less-experienced scrub nurses benefited from our approach. Error frequencies were comparable between conditions. Qualitative feedback highlighted improved process clarity, reduced information overload, and perceived independence. To summarize, our marker-free multi-camera AR guidance approach for surgical instruments can, subjectively and objectively, improve intraoperative instrumentation performance, particularly for untrained scrub nurses.


Evaluation of Visual Guidance Techniques for Augmented Reality-Supported Assembly Tasks in the Medical Context
J.Kreimeier, P. Prasad, S. Li, N. Corell, L. Theelke, C. Kleinbeck, H. Schieber, S. Weidert, S. Andress, F. Merkl, A. Martin-Gomez and D.Roth
Springer Virtual Reality, 2026

Abstract Springer

Handling manual assembly tasks is a crucial part of everyday life. Especially with surgical instruments, proficient handling is critical for flawless workflows, efficiency, and overall patient safety. Markerless object pose estimation and augmented reality (AR) visualization can guide users through the assembly process. There is a lack of understanding when combining state-of-the-art object tracking with different types of AR assembly visualizations. Our work introduces a comparison between tabletop in-situ, FoV-fixed, and world-fixed visual guidance techniques. We use a benchmark task with step-by-step assembly guidance via gaze control and reproducible 3D-printed assemblies of varying complexity and shape (proxies for surgical instruments). Our within-subject user study with 32 participants (16 scrub nurses and 16 laypersons) revealed that AR assembly guidance can match and partially outperform paper-based instructions regarding usability, user experience, and workload. Objective metrics (times and errors) were largely comparable to the paper baseline. We identified that world-fixed and FoV-fixed animations tend to overall outperform in-situ animations, which by design require sequential visual processing and whose augmentations' precision is most affected by pose estimation inaccuracies. We also contrast characteristics of expert and layperson feedback. All participants commended the gaze-driven testbed interface for step-by-step AR instructions, but also revealed needs for further optimization and individualization. Our insights on visualization placement characteristics using pose estimation and involving a spectrum of users and assemblies open a new perspective in the field and aim to foster future work towards pose estimation-driven AR assembly guidance.


MultiCam: On-the-fly Multi-Camera Pose Estimation Using Spatiotemporal Overlaps of Known Objects
S. Li, H. Schieber, K. Waldow, J. Kreimeier , B. Busam and D. Roth
IEEE Transactions on Visualization and Computer Graphics, 2026

Abstract IEEE

Multi-camera dynamic Augmented Reality (AR) applications require a camera pose estimation to leverage individual information from each camera in one common system. This can be achieved by combining contextual information, such as markers or objects, across multiple views. While commonly cameras are calibrated in an initial step or updated through the constant use of markers, another option is to leverage information already present in the scene, like known objects. Another downside of marker-based tracking is that markers have to be tracked inside the field-of-view (FoV) of the cameras. To overcome these limitations, we propose a constant dynamic camera pose estimation leveraging spatiotemporal FoV overlaps of known objects on the fly. To achieve that, we enhance the state-of-the-art object pose estimator to update our spatiotemporal scene graph, enabling a relation even among non-overlapping FoV cameras. To evaluate our approach, we introduce a multi-camera, multi-object pose estimation dataset with temporal FoV overlap, including static and dynamic cameras. Furthermore, in FoV overlapping scenarios, we outperform the state-of-the-art on the widely used YCB-V and T-LESS dataset in camera pose accuracy. Our performance on both previous and our proposed datasets validates the effectiveness of our marker-less approach for AR applications. The code and dataset are available on https://github.com/roth-hex-lab/IEEE-VR-2026-MultiCam.
